Voyager 8 upgrade

Why this is its own pillar

Most YASC attendees are already on Voyager. The Voyager 8 upgrade is the most common project they have in the next 12 months, and Virtuoso is gated behind it. Three CTS clients (GRC, Longpoint, Clear Investment Group) are mid-migration right now.

What changes in Voyager 8

  • New UI and navigation; 7S and 8 are designed to coexist during transition.
  • Report Builder and YSR become the reporting stack. Custom reports and SQL-based YSRs need to be reconciled.
  • Purpose-built dashboards for receivables, renewals, collections, and period close.
  • Virtuoso Assistant embedded in the interface.

What usually breaks

  • Custom reports, YSRs, and scheduled report packets that nobody documented.
  • Plugins, custom workflows, and notification rules.
  • COI and vendor compliance configurations.
  • Interfaces and file drops (BAI, payroll, bank, municipal APIs).
  • Security roles and menu sets.
  • User adoption: experienced 7S users resist the new screens.

Discovery questions

  • What version are you on, and is the upgrade scheduled?
  • Who is inventorying and testing your custom reports and YSRs against Report Builder?
  • How many plugins, interfaces, and scheduled jobs do you have, and who owns each?
  • Who is training the property teams on the new dashboards?
  • What happened the last time Yardi changed something under you?

Proof points

  • Voyager 8 COI conversions at two clients (affordable and commercial).
  • CIP structure migration to Voyager 8 for a multifamily investor.
  • Performance and settings audits (journal imports, ETL, nightly rebuilds) at a 51,000-home SFR operator: the same audit you want before an upgrade.
  • Full BPR documentation used to guide implementation decisions.

Sales tips for consultants who don't sell

You are not there to pitch. You are there to diagnose. People buy from the person who understood their problem fastest.

The first ten seconds

  • Read the badge before you speak: company, title, city. Pick the persona opener.
  • Stand in the aisle edge of the booth, not behind the table. Hand them the koozie as the opener, not after.
  • Never open with "Can I help you?" or "Do you know CTS?" Both get "no." Open with the booth line and a question they can't answer with no.
  • Say your name and role once, early: "I'm Claudia, I run consulting at CTS." Then stop talking about CTS.

Run the conversation

  • Talk 30%, listen 70%. If you have said more than three sentences in a row, ask a question.
  • Repeat their words back. "So the recert trackers live in four spreadsheets and Maria is the only one who knows them." That sentence is the lead form entry and the follow-up email subject.
  • Ask "what happens when it breaks?" The answer is the business impact. It is what their boss will care about when they forward your email.
  • One story, not three. Pick the closest match, tell it in 20 seconds, stop. Let them ask for more.
  • Never demo at the booth. Screens end conversations. Say "I'd rather look at yours than show you mine" and book the working session.
  • Say "I don't know" fast. Then "that's exactly what we'd figure out in the working session." Guessing at a Yardi behavior in front of a Yardi admin kills credibility.
  • Use their vocabulary, not Yardi's. If they say "the ledger" don't say "tenant AR." If they say "certs" don't say "recertifications."

Qualify without interrogating

  • Three questions tell you the tier: Who owns this today? What has been tried? What would it be worth to fix?
  • "Who else feels this?" gets you the second name at the company.
  • If they can't name a process after two tries, they are a C. Be warm, hand them the one-pager, move on.

Close and exit

  • Ask for the working session every time the pain is real. Not "keep in touch." A specific next step with a rough date.
  • Offer a choice, not a yes/no: "the week after YASC or the week after that?"
  • Write the lead down while they are still there. It signals the conversation mattered, and you will not remember it in four hours.
  • End on time. Eight to ten minutes is a good booth conversation; longer means you are being polite instead of qualifying. "I want to let you get to the next session. I'll email you tomorrow with two times."
  • If someone is monopolizing the booth, your partner steps in: "Sorry to interrupt, there's someone asking for you." Then swap.

Working in pairs

  • Greeter hands off with a sentence that carries the pain: "This is Claudia, she's the one who did the compliance dashboard. Tom's team is running certs in four spreadsheets."
  • Never both talk to the same person unless they are an A and asked a technical question.
  • The one not in conversation is scanning badges in the aisle, not on their phone.

Reading the room

  • Eyes on the prize wheel: swag visitor. Be quick, be kind, get the badge scanned, don't pitch.
  • Reading the backdrop: they are deciding whether to stop. Say one sentence and hand them the koozie.
  • Asks a specific Yardi question: A or B lead. Slow down.
  • Says "I'm just looking": "Good. What's the one session you're glad you came for?" Then find the pain in their answer.
  • Yardi employee at the booth: ask about the roadmap and the consultant network. They are intel, not leads. Be generous with time.
  • Competitor at the booth: be friendly, say nothing about clients, ask what they specialize in.

Energy and stamina

  • Three days of standing is the job. Comfortable shoes, water on the table, a real lunch away from the booth.
  • Day one is the busiest. Day three afternoon is dead; use it for target-account walks and Yardi conversations.
  • After a bad conversation, reset with the next koozie handoff. Nobody remembers the awkward one.
  • Two-minute rule: if a conversation has no pain after two minutes, it's a C. Politely close it.

What not to do

  • Do not list services. Ask questions.
  • Do not quote a price at the booth beyond the three engagement shapes and the range you agreed on.
  • Do not name a client that isn't on the confirmed list.
  • Do not promise a result. Say "at a similar client we..." and let them draw the line.
  • Do not chase people down the aisle. Let the koozie and the backdrop do the pulling.
  • Do not check your phone at the table. Sit down only in the debrief.

A 12,000-unit multifamily operator is converting from MRI/IBS to Yardi.
